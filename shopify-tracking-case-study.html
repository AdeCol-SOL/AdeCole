ECOMMERCE MEASUREMENT & ANALYTICS

Shopify Ecommerce Tracking & Consent QA

An independent public-facing audit of YangaBeauty's Shopify storefront, examining observable GA4 and Meta Pixel ecommerce events, checkout measurement, and cookie consent behaviour.

Shopify · GA4 · Meta Pixel · Browser Network Inspection · Ecommerce Tracking · Consent QA

AUDIT OBJECTIVE

Is the ecommerce customer journey being measured consistently?

The objective was to examine whether key ecommerce events could be observed across YangaBeauty's public Shopify shopping journey, from product discovery through checkout.

The review focused on GA4 and Meta Pixel event behaviour, ecommerce data parameters, and the browser's response to different cookie consent choices.

This was an independent public-facing observational audit. No store administrator, private analytics account, or backend configuration access was available.

AUDIT METHODOLOGY

From browser observations to actionable measurement insights.

01

Inspect

Examined the public Shopify storefront using browser Developer Tools and Network requests to identify observable GA4 and Meta Pixel activity.

02

Validate

Observed product views, add-to-cart interactions and checkout events, including product identifiers, values and currency parameters.

03

Compare

Compared GA4 and Meta checkout values and examined browser tracking behaviour under different cookie consent choices.

04

Recommend

Documented measurement observations, investigation priorities and recommendations for further authorised validation.

KEY FINDINGS

What the ecommerce tracking audit revealed.

GA4 ecommerce events observed

The browser dispatched view_item, add_to_cart and begin_checkout events. Product information, value and currency parameters were observed. The product value was ₦10,000 NGN.

MEASUREMENT IMPLICATION

The core product-to-checkout events were observable in browser requests. Account-side processing and reporting remain unverified.

Meta Pixel ecommerce events observed

PageView, ViewContent, AddToCart and InitiateCheckout requests were observed. Product details, currency and value parameters were present in the observed requests.

MEASUREMENT IMPLICATION

Meta ecommerce events were observable across the shopping journey, but account-side processing and attribution were not verified.

Checkout values differ across platforms

GA4 begin_checkout reported ₦10,000, while Meta InitiateCheckout reported ₦12,000. The observed cart subtotal was ₦10,000, with ₦2,000 shipping.

MEASUREMENT IMPLICATION

The difference is consistent with different checkout value definitions. Both platforms' treatment of shipping should be reviewed.

CHECKOUT MEASUREMENT

Understanding the ₦2,000 checkout value difference.

During the same checkout journey, GA4 and Meta reported different values for the checkout event.

GA4

₦10,000 NGN

The begin_checkout event reported the product subtotal without the observed shipping charge.

META PIXEL

₦12,000 NGN

The InitiateCheckout event reported a value matching the product subtotal plus ₦2,000 shipping.

Interpretation: The difference is consistent with different value definitions. Further authorised testing is needed to confirm how each platform is configured to represent checkout value.

CONSENT & TRACKING QA

Did tracking behaviour change after consent choices?

01

Before a choice

GA4 page_view and Meta PageView requests were observed before a consent choice was made.

02

Reject All

After selecting Reject All and refreshing, no requests to analytics.google.com or facebook.com/tr were observed.

03

Accept All

GA4 and Meta PageView requests were observed after selecting Accept All. However, the observed GA4 consent parameter remained gcs=G111.

04

New tab behaviour

The Accept All choice did not persist in a new tab opened through the Tag Assistant debugging session. The consent banner appeared again.

Investigation priority: Review consent defaults, consent updates and preference persistence. These observations warrant further investigation but do not independently establish a legal compliance breach.

RECOMMENDED ACTIONS

Priorities for improving ecommerce measurement reliability.

01. Review consent configuration

Validate consent defaults and updates for analytics and advertising storage. Confirm that the selected preference persists across pages and new sessions.

02. Align checkout value definitions

Document whether shipping and other charges are included in checkout values. Establish consistent measurement definitions across GA4 and Meta.

03. Validate purchase measurement

With authorised access, conduct a controlled test purchase to validate purchase events, transaction IDs, item details, currency and revenue values.

04. Review tag architecture

Confirm tag ownership, destinations and deployment methods. Document the relationship between Shopify, Google tags, GA4 and Meta Pixel.

AUDIT SCOPE & LIMITATIONS

What this assessment can—and cannot—confirm.

This assessment was based on publicly observable storefront behaviour and browser network requests. It was not a technical implementation project or an account-level audit.

What was examined

  • Public Shopify storefront interactions
  • Observable GA4 and Meta Pixel requests
  • Product and checkout event parameters
  • Browser tracking behaviour under consent choices

What was not accessible or tested

  • Shopify administrator and backend settings
  • Private GA4, Google Ads and Meta accounts
  • Server-side tracking configuration
  • Completed purchase and transaction reporting
  • Platform attribution and reporting accuracy

Browser requests confirm that events were dispatched, not that they were successfully processed, attributed or reported by the destination platforms. No legal compliance assessment was performed.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

What this audit demonstrates.

This project demonstrates how browser-level inspection can uncover meaningful ecommerce measurement questions without access to a store's private analytics accounts.

  • Identified observable GA4 and Meta Pixel ecommerce events across the shopping journey.
  • Detected a ₦2,000 difference between the two platforms' reported checkout values.
  • Compared tracking behaviour before and after cookie consent choices.
  • Converted technical observations into prioritised recommendations for further validation.

Skills demonstrated: Ecommerce measurement QA, GA4 event inspection, Meta Pixel validation, browser Network analysis, consent behaviour testing and measurement documentation.
