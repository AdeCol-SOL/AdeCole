INFLUENCER MARKETING ANALYTICS

From Engagement Patterns to Measurable Business Outcomes

An analytical study exploring influencer engagement patterns and a separate campaign measurement framework designed to connect creator activity with measurable business outcomes.

PROJECT OVERVIEW

Two Analyses. One Measurement Perspective.

Influencer marketing performance cannot be understood through engagement metrics alone. Businesses also need to understand how creator activity connects to website traffic, leads and campaign costs.

This case study approaches that challenge through two complementary analyses, each with a distinct dataset and purpose.

01. Empirical Engagement Analysis

Examines observed influencer engagement patterns, follower-size segments, outliers and correlations using statistical analysis.

02. Synthetic Campaign Measurement

Demonstrates how influencer, campaign, deliverable, traffic and conversion data can be connected to evaluate campaign performance.

PART 01 · EMPIRICAL ANALYSIS

Understanding Influencer Engagement Patterns

This analysis examines observed influencer engagement data to explore how engagement patterns vary across follower-size segments and how outliers influence the interpretation of performance.

Research Approach

The analysis combines exploratory data analysis, follower-segment comparisons, outlier detection and correlation analysis. An interquartile range (IQR) method was applied within follower-size segments to identify outliers. Bootstrap resampling was also used to assess the uncertainty around estimated correlations.

Interpreting the Results

Removing identified outliers changes the overall average engagement rate, illustrating how extreme observations can influence summary statistics.

The correlation analysis also requires caution. Evidence across the first three follower-size segments was inconclusive, while the 200M+ segment had only 17 records. Its results should therefore not be treated as conclusive evidence of a general relationship.

Analytical distinction: Engagement and correlation measures describe patterns in the observed dataset. They do not establish that follower size causes higher or lower engagement.

DATA ENGINEERING & STRUCTURE

Building a Connected Measurement Framework

The synthetic campaign measurement exercise was structured in BigQuery using five related tables. Together, these tables provide a framework for connecting influencer activity to campaign delivery, website traffic and leads.

Influencers

Stores creator-level information used to identify and analyse individual influencers.

Campaigns

Organises campaign information and provides the campaign context for measurement.

Deliverables

Records influencer campaign deliverables and connects creator activity to campaigns.

Traffic

Records tracked website visits associated with influencer activity.

Conversions

Records leads and connects conversion activity to tracked traffic and campaigns.

Data Integrity Validation

Before analysing campaign performance, SQL validation checks were used to test whether the relationships between the five tables were consistent.

Tools and Technologies

Google BigQuery was used for data storage and SQL-based analysis, while Looker Studio was used to present the results through interactive dashboards.

WHAT THE ANALYSIS REVEALS

Key Findings and Practical Implications

01. Engagement Analysis

  • The original dataset recorded an average engagement rate of 2.02%, increasing to 2.32% after identified outliers were excluded.
  • Outlier treatment changes the interpretation of aggregate engagement performance, making data quality and statistical context important.
  • Correlation evidence across the first three follower-size segments was inconclusive.
  • The 200M+ follower segment contained only 17 records, requiring particular caution when interpreting its results.

02. Campaign Measurement

  • Creator C recorded the highest visit-to-lead conversion rate at 6.0%, despite generating fewer visits than the other creators.
  • Creator B generated the highest traffic volume and lead count, but had the lowest conversion rate at 3.0%.
  • Creator A and Creator C both recorded a creator fee per lead of $12.50, compared with $16.67 for Creator B.
  • The full campaign cost per lead was $19.57, demonstrating why creator-level fees and total campaign costs should be evaluated separately.

03. The Broader Measurement Lesson

Engagement, traffic, leads and costs provide different perspectives on influencer marketing performance. A useful measurement framework should connect these dimensions rather than relying on a single metric.

The empirical analysis explores observed engagement patterns, while the synthetic campaign exercise demonstrates how downstream campaign measurement can be structured. Together, they illustrate complementary analytical capabilities.

CRITICAL EVALUATION

Limitations and Opportunities for Further Development

Empirical Engagement Analysis

  • The analysis is based on the available dataset and should not automatically be generalised to all influencers or platforms.
  • Correlation does not establish causation.
  • The limited number of observations in the 200M+ follower segment restricts the certainty of conclusions for that group.

Synthetic Campaign Measurement

  • The campaign dataset is simulated and does not represent actual advertising or client performance.
  • Revenue, return on ad spend (ROAS), lead quality and incremental impact were not measured.
  • The framework demonstrates data relationships and reporting methods, rather than proving real-world campaign effectiveness.

Potential Next Steps

Further development could introduce real campaign data, revenue attribution, lead-quality assessments, and incrementality testing. Integration with advertising and analytics platforms could also provide a more complete view of campaign performance.

These additions would help move the framework beyond descriptive reporting towards more comprehensive marketing effectiveness measurement.
